Claim #

A thrown ball's path is two independent motions: constant horizontal speed, and vertical motion under constant gravity.

Demonstration. Shows the relationship in particular cases. It does not prove it in general.

Assumptions #

  • no air resistance
  • constant gravity
  • flat ground, with launch and landing at one height
  • scene units, not SI: slow motion with g = 2.5 and a base speed of 6.32

How it was checked #

  • ball position against the closed form x = vx·t and y = 0.25 + vy·t − ½·g·t²: 35 samples, largest difference 0.002 scene units. Five flights: 30°, 45° and 60° at speed 1, and 45° at speeds 0.75 and 1.25

Transcript #

Captions are on by default in the player. Times link to that moment in the film.

  1. 0:01 Launch a ball at an angle, and it flies in a curve. #
  2. 0:05 That one curve is really two simple motions, happening at once. #
  3. 0:10 Across the ground, nothing pushes the ball sideways, so its horizontal speed never changes. Equal steps, in equal times. #
  4. 0:21 Up and down, gravity pulls on the ball, so its vertical speed changes by the same amount every second. #
  5. 0:28 It slows on the way up, stops for an instant at the top, then speeds up on the way down. #
  6. 0:35 Now orbit the path. The ground sees only the sideways motion, and the pole sees only the up and down. #
  7. 0:44 Put the two back together: at every moment, a sideways step plus an up and down step makes one step along the curve. #
  8. 0:52 Change the angle, and the same two motions make a different curve. #
  9. 0:57 Thirty and sixty degrees land in the same place. Forty-five degrees goes farthest. #
  10. 1:04 Change the speed, and the whole curve scales: faster means higher and farther. #
  11. 1:10 Independent components combine into one path. That is the whole idea. #
  12. 1:17 Made with Strata. #

What the colours mean #

The film uses colour to carry meaning. Here is what each one distinguishes.

aqua
the sideways motion: the ground line, the ball's shadow on the ground, and the sideways arrow, which never changes.
coral
the up-and-down motion: the pole, the bead on it, and the vertical arrow, which shrinks to nothing at the top.
purple
gravity, whose arrow never changes.
gold
the path: a dot every tenth of a second, a ring where the ball lands, and the arrow along the curve, which is the sum of the other two.
