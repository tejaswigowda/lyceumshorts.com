Hardy–Weinberg as a square

Biology: Hardy–Weinberg law · intermediate · 1:57 · Demonstration

Claim

With random mating, one generation of offspring comes in the proportions p² + 2pq + q², drawn as a square, and the allele frequencies p and q are unchanged afterwards.

Demonstration. Shows the relationship in particular cases. It does not prove it in general.

Assumptions

  • one gene with two alleles, A and a
  • random mating in a large (effectively infinite) population
  • no mutation, no selection, no migration, no drift
  • the film shows one round with p = 3/5 and q = 2/5 (a 5 × 5 square of tiles); it is one worked case, not the general law

How it was checked

  • the 30 A tokens and 20 a tokens span the same extents as the p and q rulers: 50 samples. Read back from the loaded scene and equal to the last digit

Transcript

Captions are on by default in the player. Times link to that moment in the film.

  1. 0:01 A gene comes in two forms. Big A has frequency p. Little a has frequency q. #
  2. 0:08 Between them they make up everything: p plus q is one. #
  3. 0:13 Every child gets one allele from each parent, at random. #
  4. 0:18 So draw a square. Along the top, one parent's alleles. Down the side, the other's. #
  5. 0:26 Cut where each side splits into p and q. #
  6. 0:30 Each piece is one kind of child, and its area is how common that kind is. #
  7. 0:36 Both parents give A: p times p. That is p squared. #
  8. 0:40 One gives A and the other gives a. There are two ways, so two rectangles: two p q. #
  9. 0:47 Both give little a: q times q. That is q squared. #
  10. 0:52 Now count the alleles in all these children. Each child carries two. #
  11. 0:58 Send every A to one bin, and every little a to the other. #
  12. 1:03 Out of fifty alleles, thirty are A, and twenty are little a. #
  13. 1:07 Measure the bins with the same two rulers that measured the parents: p, and q. They match. #
  14. 1:14 The allele frequencies have not changed. Random mating only shuffles them into new pairs. #
  15. 1:22 That holds when mating is random, with no selection, no mutation, no migration, and a large population. #
  16. 1:30 Put the pieces back together: the whole square, the whole population. Area one. #
  17. 1:37 So p squared, plus two p q, plus q squared, equals one. #
  18. 1:43 That is the Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium: p and q stay fixed, so these proportions return, generation after generation. #
  19. 1:52 Made with Strata. #

What the colours mean

The film uses colour to carry meaning. Here is what each one distinguishes.

coral
allele A, its frequency p, and the AA pieces (p²).
gold
the heterozygote Aa: the two gold pieces stacked under the middle term 2pq.
aqua
allele a, its frequency q, and the aa piece (q²).
white
labels, the cuts, and the two bins that count the alleles.
