<section>
<h2 id="claim">Claim</h2>
<p class="claim-text">In a crank-slider the piston reaches its dead centers at the ends of the stroke, and the leverage of a steady push on the piston vanishes there and is largest near the middle of the stroke.</p>
<p class="small"><strong>Demonstration.</strong> Shows the relationship in particular cases. It does not prove it in general.</p>
</section>
<section>
<h2 id="assumptions">Assumptions</h2>
<ul class="assumptions">
<li>rigid parts, no clearances, the piston line passes through the shaft</li>
<li>kinematics only: no friction, no inertia, no gas pressure</li>
<li>crank radius r = 1.2 and rod length l = 3.6 scene units, so l / r = 3; the quoted 58.6% and 1.05 r belong to that ratio</li>
<li>the steady push is a device for reading the leverage, not a model of a real cylinder</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section>
<h2 id="checked">How it was checked</h2>
<ul>
<li>crank pin, rod end, piston and leverage marker against the formulas: 27 samples, largest difference 0.006 scene units. Across all eight turns of the crank; the crank pin agrees to 0.00001</li>
</ul>
</section>

<h3>What the colours mean</h3>
<p>The film uses colour to carry meaning. Here is what each one distinguishes.</p>
<dl class="colours">
<dt>coral (red)</dt><dd>the dead centers: the rings and labels at the two ends of the stroke.</dd>
<dt>aqua</dt><dd>the crankshaft circle and the line the piston travels along.</dd>
<dt>gold</dt><dd>the leverage: the stretched line on the rod, the gap, and its marker. The gap shrinks to nothing at the dead centers.</dd>
<dt>purple</dt><dd>the steady push arrow on the piston (the lilac blocks under the cylinder are its stand).</dd>
</dl>
