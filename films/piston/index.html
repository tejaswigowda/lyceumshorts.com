Claim #

In a crank-slider the piston reaches its dead centers at the ends of the stroke, and the leverage of a steady push on the piston vanishes there and is largest near the middle of the stroke.

Demonstration. Shows the relationship in particular cases. It does not prove it in general.

Assumptions #

  • rigid parts, no clearances, the piston line passes through the shaft
  • kinematics only: no friction, no inertia, no gas pressure
  • crank radius r = 1.2 and rod length l = 3.6 scene units, so l / r = 3; the quoted 58.6% and 1.05 r belong to that ratio
  • the steady push is a device for reading the leverage, not a model of a real cylinder

How it was checked #

  • crank pin, rod end, piston and leverage marker against the formulas: 27 samples, largest difference 0.006 scene units. Across all eight turns of the crank; the crank pin agrees to 0.00001

Transcript #

Captions are on by default in the player. Times link to that moment in the film.

  1. 0:01 An engine turns a straight push into a spin, with just three parts: a piston, a connecting rod, and a crankshaft. #
  2. 0:09 The piston can only slide along its cylinder. The crank can only turn about its shaft. The rod ties the two together. #
  3. 0:19 Turn the crank one full revolution, and the piston goes out and back, once. #
  4. 0:25 The crank pin travels in a circle. The piston is held to a straight line. A rod of fixed length joins them. #
  5. 0:34 Where the piston turns around are the dead centers: top dead center, and bottom dead center. #
  6. 0:41 The distance between them is the stroke. It is exactly twice the crank's radius. #
  7. 0:48 Notice the piston is not simple back and forth. A quarter turn from the top, it is already past halfway. #
  8. 0:57 Now orbit the assembly. The crank pin sits between two webs, the rod wraps the pin, and the piston turns on the other end. #
  9. 1:07 Push the piston with a steady force. Extend the rod's line to the shaft: the gold gap is the leverage the crank gets. #
  10. 1:16 At a dead center, the line runs straight through the shaft, and the leverage is zero. Near the middle of the stroke, it is the greatest. #
  11. 1:26 That is why an engine needs a heavy flywheel: its weight carries the crank through the dead centers. #
  12. 1:33 Crank-slider kinematics: a circle, a line, and a rod of fixed length. Everything else follows. #
  13. 1:41 Made with Strata. #

What the colours mean #

The film uses colour to carry meaning. Here is what each one distinguishes.

coral (red)
the dead centers: the rings and labels at the two ends of the stroke.
aqua
the crankshaft circle and the line the piston travels along.
gold
the leverage: the stretched line on the rod, the gap, and its marker. The gap shrinks to nothing at the dead centers.
purple
the steady push arrow on the piston (the lilac blocks under the cylinder are its stand).
